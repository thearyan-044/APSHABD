## Founder launch post

INDIAN STREETWEAR HAS AN IDENTITY CRISIS.

There. I said it.

We keep putting foreign cities on our chests as if our own streets never gave us anything worth wearing.

New York. Los Angeles. Tokyo.

Cool.

But what about Bandra?

What about Mylapore? Dadar? Adyar? T Nagar?

What about the lane that raised you, the station that exhausted you, the chai spot that knows your order, and the language in which your city actually speaks?

Why should Indian streetwear keep borrowing someone else’s street?

Fuck that.

I’m building APSHABD.

An Indian streetwear label built from neighbourhood memory, local scripts, and the places that made us who we are.

Not India repackaged for tourists.

Not culture reduced to an elephant, a mandala, or a badly placed Devanagari word.

Not another logo slapped on an oversized T-shirt and called a movement.

Real neighbourhoods.

Real scripts.

Real stories.

Cut into clothes worth keeping.

We begin with Chennai and Mumbai. Then Delhi, Bangalore, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Pune—and every neighbourhood loud enough to demand its place on the map.

I don’t want APSHABD to simply sell clothes.

I want someone to see their neighbourhood on a piece and say:

“That’s mine.”

I’m Aryan Joshi, founder of APSHABD.

We’re done borrowing streets.

It’s time to wear our own.
